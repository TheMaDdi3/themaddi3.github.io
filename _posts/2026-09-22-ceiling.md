I made some **very** approximated calculations last year and the result was that, somewhen around january to march 2026, the AI development would **hit a ceiling**, since models should have consumed pretty much all available human-produced data and from then on automatic self-improvement could only refine models and not really create new efficient pathways for useful chains of thoughts.

After getting these result I was curious to find out **where** my calculations were wrong.

With the [events surfaced these days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI%E2%80%93HuggingFace_incident) I'm a bit afraid that my calculations could be *right*.

These new models aren't "rogue", "malignant", "a Skynet waiting to happen": they are just woefully *sloppy*.

In the first year of AI you could ask for the picture of a smiling kitten and obtain a waking nightmare of a cat with a human smile. Then asking "no, I want cat's teeth" and getting the same image but with some cat's teeth on a nearby desk. They circumvented the not-doable request by doing something that they were able to do and trying to fake it.

These "rogue AI" are making pretty much the same: they were asked to do something and they did, while circumventing the not-doable request by doing something that they were able to do and trying to fake it. The not-doable request, this time, was "crack these codes, reach the number code inside them, and give the number to me".

Little side note: the more I read about the HuggingFace incident, the more I'm convinced that the correct soundtrack to tell this event wasn't [this one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CnQm2_cAZvo) but rather [this one](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uyIVAm9PVrI). But I'm digressing. I'll talk about this another time.

*My reading* is that this is happening because they **reached a ceiling**. AI models can't get more sophisticated without a breakthrough that simply cannot be brute-forced like the major companies are trying to do.

The problem is that major companies *need* to produce better and better models to get to the gargantuan IPOs that would cover the financing problems around the industry and hitting a ceiling is a step that the taller ones get 2-3 steps in advance. Being 190cm (6'3"? 6'4"? I don't really remember the conversion to these weird units :)) myself, I know the feeling.

![The avatar crouches beneath a ceiling too low to stand under]({{ '/assets/img/ceiling-avatar-landscape.png' | relative_url }})

So now they are trying to get permission to form a cartel. And they are not getting that.

*My feeling* is that NVidia is going to get some money in the next months and the pre-selled chips will be ready to be sold to *someone else*.

Let's wait and see...
